01 / The world-modeling lens

The video is a trace.
The world extends beyond it.

A camera records only part of a changing world. Objects leave the frame, actions have delayed effects, and the same place can look different when revisited. A model must reason about what the video shows—and what the underlying state may be.

This survey brings video comprehension, generation, unified modeling, and video world models into one framework. It asks what each learns about the relation between observed traces and evolving world states, and what evidence supports that claim.

V

Observed video

Partial, lossy, temporally ordered observations of a world.

W

Latent temporal world

Entities, relations, and physical properties evolving over time.

st

Represented state

What the model infers and maintains. It can be incomplete or wrong.

A distinction that changes the question

Smooth frames.
Consistent world?

Temporal coherence describes local continuity in a video. World consistency asks whether one admissible world trajectory explains the whole trace.

A car can move smoothly, disappear behind a wall, and return with the wrong identity. Conversely, a hard camera cut can break visual smoothness while preserving the world.

Neither property implies the other.

02 / Four operations

Four ways to connect
video and world.

These are complementary operations, not a ranking of models. One system can read, write, share, and interact.

Video comprehension · §4

Reading the world

Infer content, structure, dynamics, physics, and causality from a partial video trace.

Explore reading literature ↗

From recognition to maintained state

Which entities are present? Where are they? How do they change—and what caused that change? Video encoders, Video-LLMs, and agents provide different ways to gather and retain this evidence.

  • Semantic, spatial, temporal, physical, and causal understanding
  • Contrastive, masked, predictive, and generative representation learning
  • Video-language modeling, test-time scaling, and video agents
  • Embodied perception, driving, and domain-specific analysis
The evaluation question

Does the answer actually depend on the video? Control for text-only, single-frame, shuffled-order, and restricted-context shortcuts.

Video generation · §5

Writing the world

Render a new video trace from a partial specification: text, images, audio, motion, or other conditions.

Explore writing literature ↗

From a condition to a visual trajectory

A prompt fixes only part of a scene. The generator resolves the remaining appearance, motion, and events while preserving the identities and constraints the condition supplies.

  • Text-, image-, video-, audio-, and motion-conditioned generation
  • Continuous latents, discrete tokens, and hybrid representations
  • Diffusion, autoregressive, masked, flow, and agentic generation
  • Control, editing, character animation, and synthetic training data
The evaluation question

Does the trace follow the condition, keep the world consistent, and support its intended use—not merely look plausible?

Unified video models · §6

Sharing the world

Couple understanding and generation through common representations, parameters, interfaces, or objectives.

Explore sharing literature ↗

From coexisting capabilities to measurable synergy

A system that both understands and generates video covers two directions. Sharing machinery is a further claim; demonstrating that the directions improve one another requires controlled evidence.

  • Discrete-token, continuous-latent, modular, and agentic architectures
  • Isolated, dual, co-learning, and synergistic learning
  • Instruction-driven editing and generative reasoning
  • Per-direction, interleaved, and cross-direction evaluation
The evaluation question

Does information flow improve reading or writing when data, capacity, and compute are matched?

Video world models · §7

Interacting with the world

Model how an established state evolves and how actions, controls, or new observations change that evolution.

Explore interacting literature ↗

From a plausible clip to a responsive world

Rollout exposes what a single clip can hide. Generated errors feed back into context; out-of-view content must persist; actions must have their intended effects.

  • Observation-space and latent-state prediction
  • Passive, action-conditioned, and interactive transition interfaces
  • Controllability, memory, responsiveness, and physical grounding
  • Data engines, policy learning, evaluation, planning, and simulation
The evaluation question

Do transitions follow controls, preserve state through interventions, and improve decisions or downstream learning?

Entry points Genie ↗V-JEPA 2 ↗WorldMem ↗

V → W and W → V are shorthand: reading estimates world properties; writing receives only a partial specification. They are not exact inverses.

04 / The evidence ladder

Stronger claims need
stronger evidence.

Across all four operations, visual plausibility is a starting point. State consistency and decision utility need additional tests.

  1. 01

    Plausible output

    Are answers, generated frames, or predicted features credible on the immediate task?

  2. 02

    Faithful to evidence or conditions

    Do answers depend on observed evidence? Do outputs respect the supplied text, reference, motion, or action?

  3. 03

    Consistent state and controlled transfer

    Test identity, layout, physics, occlusion, and interventions. For sharing, isolate cross-direction transfer under matched resources.

  4. 04

    Decision utility

    Do the representations, generated data, or rollouts improve planning, policy training, or policy evaluation?

05 / Open questions

What would move
the field forward?

Seven challenges connect better methods with tests that can reveal why they work.

Observational scale shows what co-occurs.
Interventions show what changes what.

01 Long-horizon world consistency

Memory must track how hidden state evolves, not only retrieve what was last visible.

A useful test: vary rollout length and revisit distance separately; report degradation alongside memory, latency, and action-update rate.

02 Physical and causal grounding

Changing an action or physical parameter should change the relevant outcome while leaving unrelated state intact.

A useful test: paired interventions from the same initial state; locate the first violated constraint and when it appears.

03 Measurable synergy in unified models

Doing both tasks does not establish that understanding and generation improve one another.

A useful test: compare coupled and uncoupled systems under matched data, capacity, compute, and supervision.

04 Standardized evaluation of world properties

Benchmarks use different conditions, horizons, and output spaces, so headline scores are difficult to compare.

A useful test: align state properties and interventions across operations and report the protocol, budget, failures, and downstream effects.

05 Scaling control from unlabeled video

Videos show motion but often omit the actions, units, and coordinate systems that caused it.

A useful test: assess whether inferred controls predict interventions and transfer to a declared action interface, beyond passive reconstruction.

06 Generalization across scenes and embodiments

Different cameras, bodies, and action spaces can turn an apparently reusable model into a setting-specific solution.

A useful test: separate held-out scenes from held-out embodiments and evaluate their combinations under stated adaptation budgets.

07 Stochastic worlds and rare events

Rendering a rare event does not establish how likely it is. Diverse samples can still put probability mass in the wrong places.

A useful test: use calibration, proper scoring rules, and valid-mode coverage at fixed sample budgets; report invalid outcomes separately.
